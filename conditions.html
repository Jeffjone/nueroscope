03 / THE FOUNDATIONS

One label.
Many experiences.

Get to know some of the conditions discussed in conversations about neurodiversity. Start with understanding, and stay curious about the individual.

A guide to understanding, not a diagnostic tool.

These are brief educational overviews. Neurodivergent is not a medical diagnosis, and its boundaries vary. Mental health and neurological conditions are included here to reflect the original handbook’s broader scope. A qualified professional can assess individual concerns.

9 conditions to explore

01

Autism

Autism spectrum disorder · ASDDevelopment & attention

Autism involves differences in communication, interaction, behavior, and sensory experiences. The spectrum describes varied combinations of traits and support needs.

What might this look like? +
  • Communication preferences can include direct language, less eye contact, or alternative ways of communicating.
  • Routines, focused interests, and repetitive movements may be important to a person.
  • Sensory input may feel more or less intense. Support needs can vary across settings.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Offer clear expectations, respect communication preferences, and ask about sensory adjustments.

Read the reference · NIMH ↗

02

ADHD

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorderDevelopment & attention

ADHD involves ongoing patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity, or a combination of these that affect daily life across situations.

What might this look like? +
  • People may have difficulty staying organized, starting tasks, or keeping track of time.
  • Restlessness can be visible movement or an internal feeling.
  • Symptoms begin in childhood and may continue into adulthood; presentation can change.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Break tasks into steps, make time and expectations visible, and offer movement breaks.

Read the reference · NIMH ↗

03

Dyslexia

A learning difference affecting readingLearning & movement

Dyslexia can affect accurate or fluent word reading and spelling. Reading difficulty does not tell you a person’s intelligence.

What might this look like? +
  • Connecting speech sounds to written letters can be challenging.
  • Reading and spelling may take additional time and effort.
  • People have different learning profiles and benefit from individualized instruction.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Offer accessible text, audio options, appropriate reading instruction, and time to process information.

Read the reference · American Brain Foundation ↗

04

Dyscalculia

A learning difference affecting mathematicsLearning & movement

Dyscalculia can affect understanding numbers, quantities, and arithmetic. Its effects can extend to everyday tasks involving time or measurement.

What might this look like? +
  • Estimating quantities or remembering number facts can be difficult.
  • Calculations may require more steps or support.
  • A person’s mathematical difficulties do not define their abilities in other areas.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Use concrete examples, visual aids, step-by-step instructions, and suitable calculation tools.

Read the reference · Cleveland Clinic ↗

05

Dyspraxia

Movement and coordination differencesLearning & movement

Dyspraxia is commonly used to describe difficulties with movement and coordination, often in the context of developmental coordination disorder.

What might this look like? +
  • Handwriting, dressing, or sports can require additional effort.
  • Planning and carrying out movements can be challenging.
  • The activities affected and level of support needed differ between people.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Allow time, offer alternatives to handwriting, and adapt activities to individual needs.

Read the reference · NHS ↗

06

Tourette syndrome

A neurodevelopmental tic disorderDevelopment & attention

Tourette syndrome involves repeated movements and vocalizations called tics. Tics are not deliberate misbehavior and can change over time.

What might this look like? +
  • Motor tics can include blinking or head movements.
  • Vocal tics can include sounds, words, or throat clearing.
  • Tic frequency and intensity may vary. Swearing is not a feature for everyone.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Avoid drawing unnecessary attention to tics, allow breaks, and follow the person’s preferences.

Read the reference · NINDS ↗

07

OCD

Obsessive-compulsive disorderOther related conditions

OCD involves recurring, unwanted thoughts, repetitive behaviors or mental acts, or both. Symptoms can cause substantial distress and disrupt everyday life.

What might this look like? +
  • Obsessions are intrusive thoughts, urges, or images.
  • Compulsions can be actions or mental routines used in response to distress.
  • OCD is more complex than liking things to be neat or organized.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Take distress seriously and support access to qualified mental health care.

Read the reference · NIMH ↗

08

Bipolar disorder

A condition affecting mood and energyOther related conditions

Bipolar disorder involves episodes of marked changes in mood, energy, and activity, including mania or hypomania and depression. These are more than ordinary daily mood changes.

What might this look like? +
  • Episodes can affect sleep, concentration, and daily activities.
  • Different forms of bipolar disorder have different episode patterns.
  • Professional assessment and ongoing care are important.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Avoid using “bipolar” as shorthand for changing moods; support access to care and flexibility.

Read the reference · NIMH ↗

09

Epilepsy

A neurological condition involving seizuresOther related conditions

Epilepsy involves a tendency to have recurrent seizures. Seizures can look very different between people and do not always involve convulsions.

What might this look like? +
  • Some seizures affect awareness; others involve movement or sensations.
  • A person may need time to recover after a seizure.
  • Individual care and support plans are important at school and work.
MAKE ROOM FOR DIFFERENCE

Learn the person’s seizure action plan and appropriate seizure first aid from a qualified source.

Read the reference · World Health Organization ↗
