1 / Myth

Neurodiversity only refers to autism.

Neurodiversity describes variation across human minds. Autism, ADHD, dyslexia, and many other experiences are part of this broader conversation.

Explore this idea
2 / Myth

People with the same diagnosis have the same support needs.

A shared diagnosis does not mean a shared experience. Needs, strengths, preferences, and circumstances differ from person to person.

Explore this idea
3 / Fact

A neurodiverse group can include neurotypical and neurodivergent people.

Neurodiverse describes variation within a group. Neurodivergent usually describes an individual whose functioning differs from what is considered typical.

Explore this idea
4 / Myth

ADD and ADHD are separate current diagnoses.

ADD is an older term. ADHD is the current term and includes presentations where inattention is more prominent than hyperactivity.

Explore this idea
5 / More to it

Accepting neurodivergent people means they should never receive treatment.

Acceptance, accommodations, and clinical care can coexist. Decisions about support or treatment should respect the individual’s goals, autonomy, and specific needs.

Explore this idea
6 / Myth

There is one correct way to refer to every autistic person.

Some prefer identity-first language; others prefer person-first language. Follow the individual’s preference instead of imposing a universal rule.
