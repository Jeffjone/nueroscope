01 / THE ESSENTIALS

Five ideas.
A broader perspective.

New to neurodiversity? Start here. These ideas are the foundation for understanding others and making everyday spaces more inclusive.

IN THIS CHAPTER01Diversity makes a difference.02No two minds are the same.03Support and acceptance belong together.04The words we use matter.05See the whole person.
01

Diversity makes a difference.

Different perspectives give communities more ways to solve problems, create, and connect. Inclusion means making room for those perspectives in everyday decisions.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Try asking a group how each person prefers to contribute before assigning roles.

02

No two minds are the same.

People differ in how they learn, communicate, move, and experience the world. A label can help someone find support, but it cannot tell you everything about that person.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Ask “What helps you learn?” instead of assuming that one approach works for everyone.

03

Support and acceptance belong together.

Recognizing someone’s identity can go hand in hand with recognizing disability and support needs. Listen to the individual, respect their choices, and take their challenges seriously.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Offer an adjustment without asking someone to prove how hard a task is for them.

04

The words we use matter.

Using disability labels as insults reinforces stigma. People also have different preferences for identity-first language, such as “autistic person,” and person-first language, such as “person with autism.”

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Follow the language a person uses for themself. If you are unsure, ask respectfully.

05

See the whole person.

People have interests, strengths, difficulties, and goals beyond a diagnosis. Strengths vary from person to person; no one needs an exceptional talent to deserve access and respect.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Get to know someone’s interests and aspirations, rather than focusing only on a label.
